1. The game in one paragraph

A fixed Narrator frames four comic stories. Each level is built for one comic hero (superhero pulp, noir detective, manga ninja, pop-art space hero), whom you play, and the map is themed that way. Each level is a pitch-dark floor of 8 rooms in a random layout; only the area around your torch is lit. You finish interactive tasks to fill a progress bar. The comic's villain is hiding as one of two vampires in the dark and keeps sabotaging you; the other vampire is your friend. Catch a vampire in your torchlight and choose REVEAL or KILL. After four levels you fight the Narrator in the final boss fight.

If the vampire is...REVEALKILL
The friendHe becomes your ally. He no longer fears light and helps with tasks, hints and later the boss fight.The friend dies. You lose him for the run, the Narrator reacts, and your ending and boss fight get harder.
The villainHe is unmasked and you fight him (short fight, torch beam as the weapon). Win and the sabotage stops, plus you gain a heart.The villain dies at once and the sabotage stops, but there is no reward fight and no heart bonus.

Already built and verified at Hour 12: the dark level with torch lighting and smooth 8-direction movement, a generator for random 8-room levels (4 difficulty tiers), 13 interactive task types (classic, comic, college and the original light-puzzle pages with their rule twists), a progress bar, sabotage countdowns with fix consoles, hearts, checkpoints and respawn, level and total timers, a map screen, comic UI and menus, CC0 audio, validators, unit tests and a browser build. The existing sabotage system will be driven by the villain vampire instead of by task completion.

STRETCH (only after all of the above are playable, in this order): light puzzle rooms in the world, a torch battery with recharge lamps, reactive music. Cut first if time is short: stretch items, then medals, then hero abilities, then the per-hero task skins (fallback: palette swaps only), then the boss mirror phase (fallback: a two-phase boss with torch damage only).

Fixed limits: 4 levels and 4 heroes (no more), original characters only (no existing comic heroes or villains), no multiplayer, no save slots, no level editor, no new engine features.

4. How the work is split

  • Claude Code: architecture, vampire AI and the choice system, fights, level generator, narrator event scripting, bug fixing, code review and merges.
  • Antigravity: hero and villain art, level themes, UI, shaders and transitions, audio, browser (Playwright) playtests, the itch.io page.
  • ChatGPT / Codex: tests, balance data, documentation, README and CREDITS, small isolated features.
  • Developer (Sankeerth): all design decisions, the story, hero and villain concepts, the Narrator's script, the playtests, every merge to main. Design choices are logged in docs/DESIGN_LOG.md.
  • Method: each tool works in its own git worktree and owns separate folders, a shared task board (docs/TASKS.md) tracks everything, commits are small and frequent so the history is auditable.

5. Quality, compliance and risks

AreaPlan
StabilityHeadless unit tests for the puzzle model, level validator for every puzzle, a generator test that builds 100+ levels and checks reachability, a Playwright browser run on every milestone build.
AssetsOnly free, CC0, CC BY, open-source or AI-generated assets. Everything listed in CREDITS.md and on the itch.io page. No paid assets.
AI disclosureClaude Code, Antigravity and ChatGPT/Codex are used for code, art, audio and documents. Every use is logged in docs/AI_USAGE.md. Core design and story direction are the developer's.
Code originAll game code is written inside the 100 hours. Only a generic empty Godot project and an editor plugin used for tooling (not part of the game) were present at the start; the plugin is credited.
Risk: the choice is a coin flipClues are deterministic: the friend always finishes one console unseen, the villain is always present near a console when a sabotage starts. Playtests check that testers can deduce the roles. Fallback: the first vampire met is always the friend in level 1 (tutorial).
Risk: vampire AI is hard to tuneKeep it simple (patrol, flee from light, freeze in the beam). Fallback: the villain sabotages on a fixed schedule.
Risk: scope (largest plan item)The vampire, choice and ally system is built first (Hours 12 to 55) and everything else is cut around it, never the other way round.
Risk: combat is not funBoth fights reuse existing mechanics (torch light, mirrors, hearts). Attacks are slow and readable. Playtests at Hour 45 (villain fight) and Hour 70 (boss). Fallbacks: the villain fight becomes a freeze-and-catch moment, the boss drops to two phases without mirrors.
Risk: art style workloadGenres are palette, font and caption swaps over one shared set of procedural shapes. Fallback: two genres instead of four.
Risk: web performance2D lights are limited to the hero's torch; test every milestone in a browser; keep the export under 40 MB.
Risk: AI quota limitsWork is split across three tools with a handoff note on the task board; the developer reassigns tasks to whichever tool has quota left.
