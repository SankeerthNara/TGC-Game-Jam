MIRROR PAGE

A comic-page light puzzle where the narrator breaks the rules. TGC Game Jam proposal.
TeamSankeerth Nara (solo)
Repositoryhttps://github.com/SankeerthNara/TGC-Game-Jam (public, MIT license)
ThemesComic, Twist, Light (all three, in one game)
Engine / platformGodot 4.7, GDScript, Compatibility renderer; browser (HTML5) build on itch.io
Target playtime15 minutes first playthrough (20 minutes maximum)

1. Pitch

Every level is a page of a comic book. A beam of light starts in one panel and must reach the stars on the page. You solve it by dragging whole comic panels to swap them and flipping mirrors. Because emitters, walls and targets live inside panels, rearranging the page rearranges the puzzle. An unreliable narrator speaks in caption boxes, and keeps breaking the rules of the world.

2. How the three themes work together

ThemeHow it appears in the game
ComicThe page is the puzzle. Panels are the movable pieces, gutters are rule-changing borders, narrator captions tell a story, panels shake with comic impact effects, and sound effects appear as comic words (POW, ZAP).
LightThe beam of light is the only mechanic. It reflects off mirrors, stops at walls and lights targets. It is the player's only tool and the only feedback.
TwistThe narrator twists the game itself. (1) Goal twist: after you solve a page, the caption reveals the hero you were lighting is the villain. Good and bad targets swap and you must re-solve the same page. (2) Rule twists: mirrors start lying, and the gutters start bending light.

3. Core mechanics

  • Swap panels: drag one panel onto another. Everything inside moves with it.
  • Flip mirrors: click a mirror to switch its angle (/ or \).
  • Beam: reflects off mirrors, is blocked by walls, ends on a target. A level is solved when every good target is lit and no bad target is lit.
  • Locked panels: some panels cannot be moved, which constrains the solution in later levels.
  • Undo / reset: unlimited undo, so players can experiment freely.

6. Final scope

In scope: panel swapping, mirror flipping, undo and reset, locked panels, three rule types, the goal-flip twist, narrator captions, 6 levels, main menu, pause menu, ending screen, music and sound effects, comic-style art, browser build on itch.io.

Out of scope (cut first if time runs short): level editor, save system, more than 6 levels, controller and mobile touch support, localisation.

Success criteria: a new player finishes all 6 levels in 15 to 20 minutes with no instructions beyond the in-game captions, and the web build runs without errors in a browser.

7. Technical plan

  • Architecture: a pure-logic model (PageModel, BeamSolver) separated from the view (PageView) and game flow. UI, audio and effects only listen to a global signal bus. This keeps the logic testable without running the game.
  • Levels as data: JSON files describing panels and cells. A built-in headless level validator brute-forces every panel arrangement to prove each level is solvable and reports its minimum number of moves; a level generator proposes candidate layouts.
  • Web safety: Compatibility renderer and GDScript only (required for Godot web export); no threads or native plugins. Web builds are tested in a browser regularly, not only at the end.
  • Status at proposal time: a playable core prototype already exists in the repository (panel swapping, mirrors, beam, goal-flip twist, 3 levels, validator), with placeholder visuals.

9. Assets and AI disclosure

  • Assets: only free, open-source, CC0 / CC BY or AI-generated assets. No paid assets. Every third-party asset is listed in CREDITS.md with source and license, and on the itch.io page.
  • AI tools used: Claude Code (architecture, core gameplay code, review), Antigravity (UI, art and audio generation, level authoring, browser testing) and ChatGPT / Codex (tests, documentation, small isolated features). All use is logged in docs/AI_USAGE.md.
  • Human direction: concept selection, design decisions, level and narrator writing direction and all merges are done by the developer; reasoning is recorded in the design log. Workflow and ownership between the tools is documented in AI_WORKFLOW.md.
  • Editor tooling: the open-source Godot AI plugin (MIT) is used in the editor only and is not part of the game.
